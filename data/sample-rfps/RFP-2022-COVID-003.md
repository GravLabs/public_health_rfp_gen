**RFP ID:** Public Health Labs-RFP-2022-COVID-003  
**Issue Date:** January 10, 2022  
**Federal Sponsor:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) / DDID  
**CDC Award Number:** 6NU90TP922072-03  
**Program Area:** COVID-19 Response — Genomic Surveillance

---

## 1. Background and Purpose

As the COVID-19 pandemic transitioned from acute response to sustained surveillance, federal partners identified whole-genome sequencing (WGS) of SARS-CoV-2 as a critical tool for variant detection and public health decision-making. The emergence of the Omicron variant in late 2021 underscored the need for robust, distributed genomic surveillance capacity within state public health laboratories.

Public Health Labs, in partnership with CDC's COVID-19 Genomics Program, is funding state laboratories to sustain and expand SARS-CoV-2 sequencing capacity, improve turnaround time, and contribute sequence data to GISAID and CDC's National SARS-CoV-2 Strain Surveillance (NS3) program.

## 3. Eligibility Criteria

- Current Public Health Labs member state or territorial public health laboratory
- Demonstrated SARS-CoV-2 WGS capability (at least 50 sequences contributed to GISAID or NS3 prior to application)
- CLIA high-complexity certification
- Agreement to submit sequences to CDC NS3 program on required schedule
- Not under a corrective action plan for prior genomic surveillance funding

---

## 4. Priority Areas and Scope of Work

### Priority Area A: Sequencing Throughput and Turnaround
- Achieve minimum weekly sequencing capacity of 96 samples with ≤5 day turnaround from sample receipt to GISAID submission
- Implement at least one Illumina or Oxford Nanopore sequencing platform validated for SARS-CoV-2
- Optimize bioinformatics pipelines for automated lineage calling (Pangolin, Nextclade)

### Priority Area B: Representative Sampling and Sentinel Surveillance
- Develop geographic and demographic sampling strategy to ensure representativeness across the state
- Establish formal agreements with at least 5 sentinel sites (hospitals, commercial labs) for specimen submission
- Submit weekly sequencing reports to CDC using standardized CDC template

### Priority Area C: Workforce and Bioinformatics Capacity
- Train minimum 2 FTE in bioinformatics analysis for SARS-CoV-2 WGS
- Implement Galaxy or equivalent accessible bioinformatics platform for routine analysis
- Document standard operating procedures (SOPs) for end-to-end sequencing workflow

### Priority Area D: Data Sharing and Public Health Integration
- Submit 100% of sequences to GISAID within 21 days of collection
- Participate in CDC's SC2 Strain Surveillance weekly reporting calls
- Integrate sequencing findings into state epidemiological reports within 72 hours of variant detection

---

## 5. Reporting Requirements

- **Monthly Sequencing Dashboard Reports** — standardized CDC data submission by the 5th of each month
- **Quarterly Progress Narratives** — 30 days after each quarter
- **Variant Alert Reports** — within 48 hours of detecting a WHO-designated Variant of Concern (VOC)
- **Final Performance Report** — 60 days post-period of performance

---

## 6. Budget Requirements

Allowable: sequencing reagents, bioinformatics software subscriptions, personnel (sequencing technicians, bioinformaticians), sequencing platform maintenance contracts, training. Sequencing equipment procurement allowable if laboratory does not already have validated platform.

Unallowable: PCR testing (covered under separate PHEP funding), patient care costs, construction.

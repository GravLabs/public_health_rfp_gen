## 1. Background and Purpose

HIV molecular epidemiology uses HIV sequence data to identify transmission clusters, understand epidemic dynamics, and guide targeted prevention interventions. CDC's HIV Surveillance Report now includes HIV sequence data as a core surveillance element, and the National HIV/AIDS Strategy calls for using molecular epidemiology to accelerate progress toward ending the HIV epidemic (EHE) in priority jurisdictions.

State public health laboratories perform HIV genotypic drug resistance testing and now play an expanded role in HIV molecular cluster detection. This RFP funds EHE priority jurisdiction laboratories to implement or enhance HIV molecular epidemiology capabilities, integrate sequence data with partner services and surveillance systems, and support local EHE strategies with actionable cluster intelligence.

## 3. Eligibility Criteria

- public health member laboratory in a CDC Ending the HIV Epidemic (EHE) priority jurisdiction (48 counties + 7 states + DC + Puerto Rico)
- CLIA high-complexity certification with HIV genotyping capability or commitment to implement
- Partnership with state/local HIV surveillance program (letter of support required)
- Agreement to comply with CDC HIV sequence data sharing and privacy policies

---

## 4. Scope of Work

### A. HIV Genotyping for Drug Resistance and Sequencing
- Implement or expand HIV genotypic drug resistance testing (RT, protease, integrase regions)
- Achieve ≥90% of newly diagnosed HIV cases with sequence data submitted to CDC HIV SURVEILLANCE MODULE within 90 days of diagnosis
- Implement HIV genotyping at no cost for uninsured newly diagnosed individuals in EHE jurisdictions

### B. Molecular Cluster Detection and Response
- Implement CDC MicrobeTrace or equivalent tool for HIV cluster detection and visualization
- Conduct quarterly cluster analysis and report findings to state/local HIV surveillance and partner services programs
- Develop action threshold protocol: clusters ≥5 persons growing at ≥2 new persons/quarter trigger formal rapid response review

### C. Pre-Treatment Drug Resistance Surveillance
- Conduct transmitted drug resistance (TDR) surveillance using baseline genotypes from treatment-naive individuals
- Report TDR prevalence annually to CDC and state HIV surveillance program
- Flag individuals with high-level resistance to recommended first-line regimens for expedited clinical consultation

### D. Data Integration and Privacy
- Implement HIV sequence data privacy protections per CDC guidelines (spatial masking, minimum reporting thresholds)
- Integrate genotyping results with Electronic Laboratory Reporting (ELR) within 24 hours
- Participate in CDC HIV sequence data validation exercise annually

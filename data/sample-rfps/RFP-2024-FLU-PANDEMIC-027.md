---

## 1. Background and Purpose

The COVID-19 pandemic exposed critical vulnerabilities in the national laboratory system's ability to rapidly scale from routine surveillance to pandemic response. Concurrent pandemic-level influenza outbreaks — a long-standing preparedness concern — would strain state laboratory systems handling both COVID-19 legacy demands and novel influenza surge. CDC's pandemic influenza preparedness framework requires state laboratories to maintain interoperable, scalable testing networks capable of rapid platform pivoting.

This RFP funds state laboratories to build and test interoperable influenza surge networks, validate cross-platform influenza A subtyping assays, and develop data-driven capacity planning tools that translate forecast epidemiological curves into laboratory resource projections.

## 3. Eligibility Criteria

- Public Health Labs member state laboratory performing seasonal influenza surveillance

- Willingness to enter formal mutual aid agreements with ≥2 neighboring state laboratories

---

## 4. Scope of Work

### A. Cross-Platform Assay Validation
- Validate influenza A subtyping (H1, H3, H5, H7) on at minimum 2 instrument platforms to enable surge platform failover
- Validate same-day influenza A subtyping workflow achieving results within 6 hours of specimen receipt
- Demonstrate validated results concordance ≥99% between platforms

### B. Interoperable Surge Network
- Execute formal mutual aid agreements with ≥2 neighboring state public health laboratories for specimen overflow during declared emergencies
- Conduct joint interoperability exercise with network partners at least once during period of performance, simulating 5× normal specimen volume
- Document reagent sharing protocols, chain-of-custody procedures, and electronic result transfer between network partners

### C. Capacity Planning Tools
- Develop or implement laboratory capacity planning dashboard linking CDC flu forecast models to projected specimen volumes, reagent consumption, and staffing requirements
- Validate dashboard projections against actual surge data from 2022–2023 or 2023–2024 influenza season
- Share dashboard methodology with Public Health Labs network for broader adoption

### D. Sentinel Surveillance Expansion
- Add at minimum 3 new sentinel surveillance sites (emergency departments, urgent care networks, or LTC facilities) contributing specimens to state influenza surveillance
- Implement electronic specimen manifest eliminating paper requisitions from all sentinel sites within period of performance

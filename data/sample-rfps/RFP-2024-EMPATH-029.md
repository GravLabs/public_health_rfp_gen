**RFP ID:** Public Health Labs-RFP-2024-EMPATH-029  
**Issue Date:** July 1, 2024  
**Federal Sponsor:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) / CSELS  
**CDC Award Number:** 6NU50CK000591-05  
**Program Area:** Emerging Threats — Unknown Etiology Surveillance

---

## 1. Background and Purpose

COVID-19 was not the first and will not be the last novel pathogen to emerge with pandemic potential. Preparedness requires that state public health laboratories maintain capacity to rapidly characterize specimens from patients with respiratory, neurological, or hemorrhagic illness of unknown etiology — to either rule out known pathogens or detect novel agents requiring escalation to CDC.

This RFP funds a state sentinel surveillance network for illness of unknown etiology, grounded in next-generation sequencing (metagenomic or agnostic sequencing), supported by broad respiratory and encephalitis panels, and integrated with clinical and epidemiological partners capable of identifying unusual illness clusters early.

## 3. Eligibility Criteria

- Public Health Labs member state or large local public health laboratory with NGS capability
- BSL-2+ containment for handling clinical specimens of unknown etiology
- Active connection to at least one academic medical center with Infectious Disease and Neurology departments serving as sentinel clinical sites
- Willingness to share all metagenomic sequence data with CDC under a data sharing agreement

---

## 4. Scope of Work

### A. Broad Respiratory and Encephalitis Testing Panels
- Implement or validate a comprehensive respiratory panel (minimum 25 pathogens) by multiplex PCR covering known respiratory viruses, atypical bacteria, and fungal pathogens
- Implement encephalitis/meningitis panel (minimum 15 pathogens) including CSF-appropriate matrices
- Establish 24-hour rapid panel testing capability for sentinel site ICU referrals

### B. Metagenomic/Agnostic Sequencing for Unknown Etiology
- Implement metagenomic next-generation sequencing (mNGS) workflow capable of unbiased pathogen identification from respiratory, CSF, and serum matrices
- Validate against at least 10 known positive specimens before clinical deployment
- Develop bioinformatics pipeline for automated pathogen identification, contamination removal, and novel sequence flagging with ≤48-hour time-to-result

### C. Sentinel Site Network
- Establish formal sentinel surveillance agreements with minimum 3 clinical sites (hospitals with ICUs)
- Define case inclusion criteria for submission: ICU patients with febrile illness of unknown etiology after standard workup, OR epidemiologically unusual illness clusters
- Achieve testing for ≥80% of eligible sentinel site referrals within period of performance

### D. Escalation Protocol
- Develop and test escalation protocol for specimens with sequences suggesting novel or highly unusual pathogens
- Establish direct communication channel with CDC Emergency Operations Center (EOC) for escalation ≤2 hours of detection
- Conduct simulation exercise of novel pathogen detection and escalation annually

---

## 5. Reporting Requirements

- **Monthly Surveillance Summary** — eligible referrals, testing completed, etiologies identified
- **Novel Pathogen Alerts** — within 2 hours of detection to CDC EOC
- **Exercise After-Action Reports** — annual
- **mNGS Sequence Submissions** — to CDC BioProject within 30 days of analysis
- **Final Report** — 90 days post-period of performance

## 6. Budget Requirements

## Environmental Health Laboratory Capacity — Climate-Attributable Disease Surveillance

**Federal Sponsor:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) / NCEH

**Program Area:** Environmental Health — Climate and Health

---

## 1. Background and Purpose

Climate change is driving increased incidence of environmentally mediated diseases — including harmful algal blooms (HABs), waterborne disease outbreaks, vector-borne illnesses, and heat-related chemical exposures. Public health laboratories are uniquely positioned to provide the environmental testing infrastructure that links environmental exposures to health outcomes, but few state laboratories have maintained or expanded environmental health testing capacity following decades of resource decline.

This RFP funds state public health laboratories to develop or expand environmental health testing capacity directly relevant to climate-attributable disease surveillance, with an emphasis on integrating environmental and clinical laboratory data to close the exposure-to-outcome evidence gap.

## 3. Eligibility Criteria

- public health member laboratory with existing environmental testing capability (water, soil, or air)
- Partnership with state environmental agency or EPA regional office (letter of support required)
- CLIA certification and/or NELAP accreditation for environmental methods
- Demonstrated ability to link environmental and clinical data systems

Priority given to laboratories in states with documented high climate vulnerability (NOAA Climate Vulnerability Index tier 3 or 4) or recent HAB or waterborne disease outbreaks.

---

## 4. Priority Areas and Scope of Work

### Priority Area A: Harmful Algal Bloom (HAB) Toxin Testing
- Validate and implement LC-MS/MS methods for cyanotoxins (microcystins, cylindrospermopsin, saxitoxins) in recreational and drinking water
- Establish turnaround time target of ≤72 hours from sample receipt for HAB toxin analysis
- Integrate HAB testing results with state drinking water surveillance and recreational water advisory systems

### Priority Area B: Waterborne Pathogen Surveillance
- Implement wastewater surveillance for at least 3 priority climate-sensitive pathogens (e.g., Vibrio, Cryptosporidium, enteric viruses)
- Develop specimen processing protocols compatible with both clinical and environmental matrices
- Link wastewater surveillance data to CDC NWSS (National Wastewater Surveillance System)

### Priority Area C: Exposure-Outcome Data Integration
- Develop data linkage methodology connecting environmental testing results to clinical case reports for at least 2 exposure-outcome pairs
- Implement GIS-enabled reporting dashboard for environmental test results with spatial correlation to health outcome data
- Submit quarterly integrated environmental-health data reports to CDC NCEH

---

## 5. Reporting Requirements

- **Quarterly Environmental Testing Reports** — volumes, species/analytes, turnaround times
- **Semi-Annual Exposure-Outcome Integration Reports** — data linkage results and case findings
- **HAB Event Reports** — within 48 hours of confirmed toxin detection above action levels
- **Final Report** — 90 days post-period of performance including sustainability plan

## 6. Budget Requirements

Environmental testing equipment and reference materials allowable. GIS software licenses allowable. Data integration consultant costs allowable if procured competitively. Personnel must include at least one environmental scientist at 30% FTE minimum. Partnership with EPA or state environmental agency does not allow Public Health Labs funds to flow to the environmental agency — those relationships must be in-kind or separately funded.

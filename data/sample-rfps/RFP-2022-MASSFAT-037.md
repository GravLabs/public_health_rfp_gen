## Mass Fatality DNA Identification — Disaster Victim Identification Laboratory Capacity
**RFP ID:** Public Health Labs-RFP-2022-MASSFAT-037  
**Issue Date:** June 20, 2022  
**Federal Sponsor:** Department of Justice (DOJ) / National Institute of Justice (NIJ)  
**NIJ Award Number:** 2022-NIJ-014788  
**Program Area:** Forensic Science — Mass Fatality Identification

---

## 1. Background and Purpose

Mass fatality incidents — wildfires, building collapses, transportation disasters, and mass violence events — increasingly challenge state forensic and public health laboratory systems to perform rapid DNA-based victim identification at scale. The Camp Fire (2018, 85 deaths), the Champlain Towers collapse (2021, 98 deaths), and multiple wildfire seasons have strained state capacity and demonstrated that most state forensic laboratories lack the rapid DVI (Disaster Victim Identification) protocols, reference sample processing pipelines, and CODIS integration pathways required for large-scale identification. This RFP, administered through Public Health Labs in coordination with NIJ, funds state public health and forensic laboratories to build and validate rapid mass fatality DNA identification capability.

## 3. Eligibility Criteria

- Public Health Labs member state laboratory OR accredited state forensic laboratory with DNA capability
- CODIS (Combined DNA Index System) participating laboratory with FBI-approved CODIS access
- CLIA high-complexity certification (for public health laboratories)
- Participation in state mass fatality response plan or state Disaster Mortuary Operational Response Team (DMORT) coordination

---

## 4. Scope of Work

### A. Rapid DVI DNA Processing
- Implement or validate STR typing (minimum 20-locus CODIS core panel) for degraded specimens from mass fatality scenarios using validated kits (GlobalFiler or equivalent)
- Achieve processing of 50 unique degraded samples from remains within 72 hours of incident declaration
- Implement validated bone extraction protocol (DNAIQC or equivalent) for challenging specimen types

### B. Reference Sample Processing and Family Reference Database
- Establish rapid processing workflow for buccal reference samples from family members of missing persons
- Implement CODIS Missing Persons module integration for mass fatality case management
- Achieve 24-hour processing time for family reference samples during active DVI operation

### C. mtDNA for Highly Degraded Specimens
- Implement or contract mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) sequencing capability for specimens where nuclear DNA recovery is insufficient
- Partner with FBI Laboratory or qualified commercial mtDNA laboratory if in-house capability not feasible
- Document mtDNA referral pathway in laboratory SOP

### D. DVI Exercises and Protocols
- Develop written mass fatality DNA identification SOP reviewed by state medical examiner and FBI CODIS administrator
- Participate in at least one regional DMORT or state mass fatality tabletop exercise annually
- Train minimum 4 laboratory staff in DVI specimen handling, chain of custody, and CODIS upload protocols

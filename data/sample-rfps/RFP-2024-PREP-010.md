# REQUEST FOR PROPOSALS
## Next-Generation Emergency Preparedness: AI-Assisted Surge Decision Support for Public Health Laboratories
**RFP ID:** Public Health Labs-RFP-2024-PREP-010  
**Issue Date:** June 3, 2024  
**Federal Sponsor:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) / OPHPR  
**CDC Award Number:** 6NU90TP922072-10  
**Program Area:** Emergency Preparedness — Laboratory Innovation

---

## 1. Background and Purpose

Post-COVID analyses documented a consistent failure mode across state public health laboratories: the inability to scale decision-making as fast as testing demand during surge events. Laboratory directors and supervisors faced overwhelming data — volumes, reagent inventories, staffing gaps, turnaround time degradation — without tools to synthesize that information into actionable surge decisions.

This RFP funds a novel category of laboratory emergency preparedness investment: technology and process innovation to enhance laboratory situational awareness and decision support during public health emergencies. Funded projects may include dashboard development, predictive modeling for reagent and capacity planning, AI-assisted quality review, or other innovations that address documented surge decision gaps.

This is an explicitly innovation-forward funding opportunity. Projects are expected to pilot approaches that — if successful — inform broader Public Health Labs and CDC guidance for laboratory emergency preparedness.

## 3. Eligibility Criteria

- public health member laboratory — state, territorial, or large local public health laboratory
- Demonstrated leadership role in prior emergency response (COVID-19, mpox, or other event)
- Commitment of laboratory director to serve as Principal Investigator and be available for cross-network learning sessions
- Institutional Review Board (IRB) determination letter or exemption documentation if project involves data from individual patient records

Applications may be submitted by consortia of up to 3 laboratories; one must be designated lead with fiscal responsibility.

---

## 4. Scope of Work

Projects must address a clearly documented gap in emergency preparedness decision-making and must include:

### Required Components (All Awardees)

**A. Problem Definition and Baseline Documentation**  
- Document the specific decision-making gap being addressed (e.g., reagent shortage prediction, surge staffing optimization)
- Establish quantitative baseline metrics that the innovation will improve
- Conduct structured interviews with laboratory leadership to validate the gap

**B. Technology or Process Innovation Development**  
- Develop or adapt a technology solution or process innovation addressing the documented gap
- If AI/ML components are used: document model training data sources, validation approach, and human oversight mechanism
- Implement data privacy protections appropriate to data types used

**C. Pilot and Evaluation**  
- Pilot the innovation in at least one simulated or real emergency response scenario within the period of performance
- Measure impact against baseline metrics established in Component A
- Document lessons learned and failure modes

**D. Knowledge Transfer**  
- Produce implementation guide suitable for adoption by other public health member laboratories
- Present findings at Public Health Labs Annual Meeting or equivalent
- Submit final report including code, data, or process documentation in open/reusable format

## 5. Reporting Requirements

## 6. Budget Requirements

Technology development costs (software, cloud services, data infrastructure) are allowable. Consultant costs for AI/ML development allowable if procured competitively. Personnel including data scientists or informatics staff allowable. Travel to Public Health Labs learning network convenings required and allowable (estimate 2 trips).

AI model API costs (e.g., OpenAI, Azure AI) allowable as operating costs if directly tied to the project.

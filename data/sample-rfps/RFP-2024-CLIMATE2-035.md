**Federal Sponsor:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) / NCEH  
**CDC Award Number:** 6NU38EH001340-03  
**Program Area:** Environmental Health — Climate and Extreme Weather

---

## 1. Background and Purpose

Climate change is driving measurable increases in heat-related illness, wildfire smoke exposure, flooding-associated waterborne disease, and post-disaster toxic exposures. The 2023 summer saw record-breaking heat waves, unprecedented wildfire smoke events affecting air quality across the northeastern United States, and multiple flooding events releasing industrial and agricultural contaminants into drinking water sources. State public health laboratories must expand beyond traditional infectious disease surveillance to provide the environmental and biomonitoring testing that quantifies climate-attributable exposures and health impacts.

---

## 4. Scope of Work

### A. Heat Illness Biomarker Surveillance
- Implement standardized heat illness biomarker panel: creatinine (kidney injury), CK (rhabdomyolysis), liver enzymes (hepatotoxicity), electrolytes for patients presenting to EDs during heat events
- Establish rapid turnaround (≤2 hours) for heat-illness panels during heat emergencies
- Contribute heat illness biomarker data to CDC BRACE (Building Resilience Against Climate Effects) surveillance

### B. Wildfire Smoke Exposure Assessment
- Implement urinary PAH (polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon) biomonitoring panel for populations exposed to wildfire smoke
- Partner with state air quality program to identify community cohorts in high smoke exposure zones for biomonitoring
- Contribute data to CDC Wildfire Health Effects Research (WHER) collaborative

### C. Flood and Disaster Contamination Response
- Develop rapid response environmental testing capability for flood-contaminated drinking water: coliform, nitrates, VOCs, metals, and PFAS
- Achieve 24-hour activation of environmental contamination testing panel following a flood or disaster declaration
- Establish pre-positioned sampling supplies cache for emergency environmental response

### D. Climate Surveillance Integration
- Develop integrated climate-health surveillance report linking environmental monitoring data with clinical and mortality outcomes
- Publish annual climate-health laboratory data report for state policymakers

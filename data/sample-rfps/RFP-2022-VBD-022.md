## 1. Background and Purpose

Vector-borne diseases are expanding geographically in the United States due to climate change driving range expansion of Aedes aegypti, Aedes albopictus, Ixodes scapularis, and other vectors. West Nile virus (WNV) remains endemic in all 48 contiguous states. Lyme disease case counts have increased 320% since 1992. Dengue, chikungunya, and Zika virus importation and local transmission events continue to stress laboratory capacity in endemic and border states. Powassan virus, ehrlichiosis, and anaplasmosis are expanding north with tick habitat.

State public health laboratories are essential for confirming suspected vector-borne disease cases, providing surge capacity during outbreak events, and participating in national ArboNET and TickNET surveillance systems.

## 3. Eligibility Criteria

- Public Health Labs member state or territorial public health laboratory
- RT-PCR capability for at minimum West Nile virus and dengue virus detection
- CLIA high-complexity certification
- Active ArboNET or TickNET reporting participant
- BSL-2 containment for arboviral diagnostics

---

## 4. Scope of Work

### A. Arboviral Diagnostic Panel Expansion
- Validate or implement RT-PCR assays for: WNV, dengue 1-4, chikungunya, Zika, St. Louis encephalitis, Eastern equine encephalitis (EEE)
- Implement IgM antibody testing for WNV and dengue by ELISA with ≤48-hour turnaround
- Maintain 24-hour response capability for suspected neuroinvasive arboviral disease during transmission season

### B. Tick-Borne Pathogen Testing
- Implement multiplexed PCR panel for Borrelia burgdorferi, Anaplasma phagocytophilum, Ehrlichia chaffeensis, Rickettsia rickettsii, and Babesia microti
- Process tick removal specimens from physicians on request with ≤5-day turnaround
- Report all confirmed cases to state epidemiology and CDC TickNET within 24 hours

### C. Seasonal Surge Preparedness
- Develop pre-season surge plan (April activation) for peak mosquito/tick season (June–October)
- Maintain standing reagent inventory for arboviral diagnostics equivalent to 200 tests per pathogen
- Participate in annual Public Health Labs vector-borne disease tabletop exercise

### D. Climate Adaptation Surveillance
- Analyze case distribution data annually for evidence of geographic range expansion within state
- Report novel or unusual vector-borne detections to CDC DVBD within 24 hours

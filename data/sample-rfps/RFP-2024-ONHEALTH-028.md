**Program Area:** One Health — Zoonotic Disease Surveillance

---

## 1. Background and Purpose

COVID-19, H5N1 avian influenza in dairy cattle, mpox, and multiple STEC outbreak investigations linked to animal exposures demonstrate that the majority of emerging infectious disease threats originate at the human-animal-environment interface. The One Health approach — integrating human, animal, and environmental health surveillance — is essential for early detection of zoonotic threats before they reach pandemic scale.

State public health laboratories currently operate largely in isolation from state veterinary and agricultural laboratories. This RFP funds integrated One Health laboratory partnerships, establishing shared data systems, cross-trained staff, and joint investigation protocols that break down the silos between human public health and animal health laboratory systems.

## 3. Eligibility Criteria

- Public Health Labs member state public health laboratory
- Formal partnership with the state veterinary diagnostic laboratory (USDA-APHIS accredited) — co-application or formal MOU required
- Joint application is strongly preferred; one laboratory must be designated lead with fiscal responsibility
- CLIA certification for human specimen testing; USDA APHIS accreditation for animal specimen component

---

## 4. Scope of Work

### A. Shared Laboratory Protocols
- Develop joint standard operating procedures for at least 3 priority zoonotic pathogens (selected from: Brucella, Leptospira, Q fever, Rabies, STEC, Salmonella, Influenza A zoonotic strains, HPAI)
- Validate cross-referral protocols for human-animal specimen pairs from the same exposure event
- Conduct at least 2 joint proficiency exercises between the public health and veterinary laboratory

### B. Integrated Data System
- Implement or adapt a shared data portal allowing bidirectional result sharing between human and animal surveillance systems
- Achieve near-real-time (≤24 hours) data exchange for all matched human-animal outbreak investigation specimens
- Integrate data with state epidemiology and state animal health authority situational awareness systems

### C. One Health Investigation Capacity
- Develop and test a One Health Outbreak Investigation Protocol covering triggers, roles, specimen collection, and reporting for human-animal exposure events
- Conduct at least one tabletop exercise simulating a zoonotic disease outbreak with joint human-animal investigation
- Designate trained One Health investigation teams at both the public health and veterinary laboratory

### D. Workforce Cross-Training
- Cross-train minimum 2 staff from each laboratory in the partner institution's specimen handling, safety, and testing protocols
- Develop shared One Health laboratory curriculum for use in regional public health and veterinary training programs

## 5. Reporting Requirements

- **Quarterly Joint Progress Reports** — co-signed by both laboratory directors
- **Exercise After-Action Reports** — within 60 days
- **Data System Integration Milestone** — notification at 12 months
- **Final Report** — 90 days post-period of performance with sustained partnership plan

## 6. Budget Requirements

Costs may be split between the public health and veterinary laboratory partners per agreed budget. Cross-training travel allowable. Joint data system development allowable. Neither laboratory may use these funds for routine animal testing services unrelated to One Health surveillance goals.

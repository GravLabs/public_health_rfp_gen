---

## 1. Background and Purpose

The opioid overdose epidemic has killed over 800,000 Americans since 1999 and accelerated sharply during the COVID-19 pandemic, with synthetic opioids — primarily illicit fentanyl and its analogs — now driving the vast majority of overdose deaths. The illicit drug supply has become increasingly complex, with fentanyl analogs, stimulant-opioid combinations, and novel psychoactive substances (NPS) appearing in toxicology screens and drug checking services.

State public health laboratories are critical nodes in overdose surveillance, performing postmortem toxicology, ante-mortem confirmatory testing, and drug supply monitoring through drug checking services (DCS). This RFP funds laboratories to modernize toxicology capacity to detect emerging substances, integrate with state overdose surveillance systems, and establish or expand drug checking services for harm reduction programs.

## 3. Eligibility Criteria

- Public Health Labs member state or large local public health laboratory with forensic or clinical toxicology capability
- LC-MS/MS or GC-MS/MS capability for comprehensive drug panel analysis
- CLIA certification (forensic toxicology under CLIA or equivalent accreditation)
- Partnership with state medical examiner/coroner office or overdose surveillance program

---

## 4. Scope of Work

### A. Emerging Substance Detection
- Implement a comprehensive illicit drug panel including fentanyl and ≥20 fentanyl analogs, xylazine (tranq), metonitazene and other nitazenes, and common adulterants
- Update analytical panel quarterly based on emerging drug supply intelligence from DEA and state law enforcement
- Achieve LOD of ≤1 ng/mL for fentanyl analogs in blood matrices

### B. Drug Checking Services
- Establish or expand drug checking services accepting anonymous drug samples from harm reduction programs
- Provide rapid fentanyl strip testing (qualitative) same-day with full LC-MS/MS confirmation within 72 hours
- Report drug supply intelligence findings (aggregate, de-identified) to state overdose surveillance program monthly

### C. Postmortem and Clinical Toxicology Integration
- Integrate toxicology results with death certificate data through state vital statistics linkage
- Participate in CDC's State Unintentional Drug Overdose Reporting System (SUDORS) data submission
- Achieve ≤21-day turnaround for postmortem toxicology for overdose deaths

### D. Near-Real-Time Surveillance
- Implement dashboard reporting of drug supply findings with ≤72-hour lag from specimen receipt
- Alert state overdose surveillance program within 24 hours of detecting a novel or high-risk substance (e.g., nitazene, carfentanil)

## 5. Reporting Requirements

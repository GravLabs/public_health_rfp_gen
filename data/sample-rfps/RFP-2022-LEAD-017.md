## Lead Testing Equity — Environmental Health Laboratory Capacity for Childhood Lead Surveillance
**RFP ID:** Public Health Labs-RFP-2022-LEAD-017  
**Issue Date:** March 14, 2022  
**Federal Sponsor:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) / NCEH  
**CDC Award Number:** 6NU38EH001333-01  
**Program Area:** Environmental Health — Childhood Lead Poisoning Prevention

---

## 1. Background and Purpose

Childhood lead poisoning remains a preventable environmental health crisis disproportionately affecting low-income communities and communities of color. The CDC Blood Lead Reference Value (BLRV) was lowered to 3.5 μg/dL in 2021, requiring higher analytical sensitivity from blood lead testing laboratories. Simultaneously, the Flint water crisis and subsequent investigations have renewed attention to environmental lead sources including drinking water, soil, and paint dust.

State public health laboratories play a critical role in providing low-cost or no-cost blood lead testing for children without insurance coverage and in conducting environmental lead testing linked to childhood lead poisoning investigations. This RFP funds laboratory capacity to meet the new BLRV analytical requirements, expand testing access in underserved areas, and integrate blood lead data with environmental investigation workflows.

## 3. Eligibility Criteria

- Public Health Labs member state or large local public health laboratory with blood lead testing capability
- Participation in CDC CLIA-mandated proficiency testing for blood lead (passing score required)
- CLIA certification for moderate or high-complexity testing (blood lead)
- Partnership with state childhood lead poisoning prevention program (letter of support required)
- NELAP accreditation for environmental lead testing OR commitment to obtain within 18 months

---

## 4. Scope of Work

### A. BLRV Analytical Upgrade
- Validate or upgrade analytical method to achieve LOD ≤1.0 μg/dL for blood lead (required to reliably detect BLRV of 3.5 μg/dL)
- Implement ICP-MS or graphite furnace AAS for blood lead if not already in use
- Complete validation documentation per Public Health Labs guidance on method validation for blood lead at lower levels

### B. Environmental Lead Testing Capacity
- Implement or expand environmental lead testing for wipe samples, paint chips, soil, and drinking water
- Achieve NELAP accreditation for environmental lead methods (Pb by ICP-MS or FLAA per EPA Method 200.8 or 7000B)
- Establish 72-hour turnaround for environmental samples linked to active childhood lead poisoning investigations

### C. Testing Access and Health Equity
- Provide blood lead testing at no charge for children ≤72 months without insurance coverage
- Establish specimen collection partnerships with at least 3 community health centers or WIC sites in high-risk census tracts
- Report quarterly on testing volumes stratified by ZIP code linked to Social Vulnerability Index

### D. Data Integration
- Achieve bidirectional data exchange with state childhood lead poisoning prevention registry
- Implement electronic result reporting for all blood lead results within 24 hours of finalization

---

## 5. Reporting Requirements

- **Quarterly Testing Volume Reports** — blood lead and environmental, stratified by SVI
- **BLRV Detection Reports** — all results ≥3.5 μg/dL reported to state program within 24 hours
- **Environmental Investigation Response Logs** — quarterly
- **Final Report** — 90 days post-period of performance

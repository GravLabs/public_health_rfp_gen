# REQUEST FOR PROPOSALS
## Emergency Genomic Sequencing Surge Infrastructure — Cross-Pathogen Rapid Response WGS
**RFP ID:** Public Health Labs-RFP-2024-GENOMICSURGE-050  
**Issue Date:** December 2, 2024  
**Federal Sponsor:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) / CSELS / OID  
**CDC Award Number:** 6NU50CK000604-SURGE  
**Program Area:** Genomic Surveillance — Emergency Surge Capacity

---

## 1. Background and Purpose

COVID-19 demonstrated that rapid whole genome sequencing (WGS) of emerging pathogens at scale is a cornerstone of public health response — variant tracking, outbreak investigation, vaccine effectiveness monitoring, and treatment selection all depend on real-time genomic surveillance. Building on the COVID-19 sequencing surge (which produced over 7 million SARS-CoV-2 sequences from U.S. laboratories), this RFP funds state public health laboratories to establish durable, pathogen-agnostic genomic surge infrastructure capable of achieving high-throughput WGS across any priority pathogen within 72 hours of emergency declaration. This cross-cutting infrastructure directly supports all pathogen-specific surveillance programs (influenza, SARS-CoV-2, mpox, foodborne pathogens, AMR organisms, novel emerging threats) through a shared sequencing platform.

## 3. Eligibility Criteria

- Public Health Labs member state public health laboratory with existing WGS infrastructure (Illumina MiSeq or higher; or Oxford Nanopore MinION or GridION)
- CLIA high-complexity certification
- Active PulseNet, SPHERES, or equivalent CDC genomic network participant
- Demonstrated WGS throughput: ≥200 genomes submitted to a CDC genomic database in the prior 12 months
- Bioinformatics capacity: dedicated bioinformatician or access to cloud-based pipeline for downstream analysis

---

## 4. Scope of Work

### A. Sequencing Platform Capacity Expansion
- Achieve minimum 500 genome equivalents/week surge capacity through platform upgrade or addition of sequencing instruments
- Implement or validate Oxford Nanopore rapid sequencing workflow (GridION or PromethION) achieving complete pathogen genome within 6 hours of library preparation for novel emerging threat scenarios
- Maintain cold-standby sequencing capacity (validated reagent stockpile, instrument PM current) for 90-day uninterrupted surge operation

### B. Pathogen-Agnostic Protocol Library
- Develop validated sequencing protocols for all priority pathogens not yet covered by existing SOPs: minimum additions of MPXV (monkeypox), H5N1 influenza, novel enterovirus, Burkholderia (Tier 1), and unknown novel pathogen (tiled amplicon approach)
- Implement adaptive sequencing decision tree: criteria-based protocol selection for any new priority pathogen request from CDC within 14 days of assay design materials receipt
- Participate in CDC AMD (Advanced Molecular Detection) protocol validation network for new pathogen sequencing methods

### C. Cloud Bioinformatics Infrastructure
- Implement or migrate all bioinformatic analysis pipelines to cloud-based infrastructure (AWS GovCloud, Azure Government, or STRIDES-compliant) with elasticity to handle 10× routine volume
- Achieve automated raw-reads-to-submission pipeline for all active pathogen surveillance programs: SARS-CoV-2, influenza, Salmonella, Listeria, STEC
- Implement data governance framework for genomic data: access controls, retention, privacy protection for specimen metadata

### D. Surge Activation and Mutual Aid
- Develop written Emergency Genomic Surge Activation Protocol with defined trigger criteria, staffing cascade, and reagent activation thresholds
- Participate in Public Health Labs regional mutual aid genomic sequencing agreement: commit to accepting overflow specimens from ≥2 neighboring state laboratories during their surge events
- Conduct annual full-scale genomic surge exercise: simulate 3× normal volume for 5 consecutive days, document gaps and corrective actions
